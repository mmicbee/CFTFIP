About the project

One project, built around a community's future.

CFTFIP is an integrated community development initiative in Nyalenda A, Kisumu East. It connects food security, economic opportunity, environmental care, and child protection so that progress in one area strengthens the others.

Why it exists

Food, income, environment, and safety are connected.

Kenyan agriculture has modernized in pockets, but many small farmers still face tools that are too technical or too expensive. Food insecurity, low yields, youth unemployment, environmental pressure, and the vulnerability of children cannot be treated as separate problems.

CFTFIP is designed as one response. A stronger local food system supports families and markets. Better livelihoods give young people options. Environmental work protects the land that the community depends on. Advocacy and child-protection work help ensure that progress reaches the people most at risk.

"The solutions offered are rarely built for the farmer actually holding the hoe."
The four commitments

Four plots, one field.

Each program has a clear focus and a shared responsibility to the others.

01

Food security

Improve crop and livestock productivity, access to appropriate technology, and nutrition practices for children and expectant mothers.

02

Economic development

Strengthen small and medium enterprises and create practical pathways into entrepreneurship and self-employment for young people.

03

Environment and climate

Protect natural resources through tree nurseries, renewable energy, drought-tolerant seeds, soil conservation, and rainwater harvesting.

04

Lobbying and advocacy

Promote accountable leadership and confront child labour, child marriage, female genital mutilation, and sexual exploitation.

Where we work

Nyalenda A, Kisumu East

The project site is set in Nyalenda A, within Kisumu East Sub-county, one of Kisumu's densely populated informal settlements. The community faces food security, income, and environmental pressures at close quarters.

WHY HERE

Informal settlements like Nyalenda A are where child exploitation and abuse are most rampant, and where poverty and neglect push children hardest toward risk. That is why the location matters to both the advocacy and food-security work.
