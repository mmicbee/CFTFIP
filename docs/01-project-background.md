# Project Background

## What is CFTFIP?

CFTFIP (Care For The Future Integrated Project) is a community development proposal targeting Nyalenda A, Kisumu EAst, in Kisumu County, Kenya. It is designed as an **integrated** project — meaning its four focus areas are meant to reinforce each other rather than run as separate initiatives.

## Why this location

Nyalenda A was chosen deliberately:

- It has a strong population of small-scale farmers who stand to benefit directly from agricultural support.
- It is a rural, remote sub-county — the areas where child exploitation and abuse are most prevalent, and where food security depends almost entirely on local production rather than imported supply.

## The problem

- Kenya's agricultural sector contributes roughly 20% of GDP and employs a large share of the population, but climate change and stalled modernization are eroding productivity.
- Around half the population lives below the poverty line.
- Maize yields in the region sit well below the national average.
- Chronic malnutrition and stunting affect close to half of children in the area.
- Youth unemployment, income inequality, and corruption slow down any reform effort.
- Many existing agricultural interventions fail because they are too technical or too expensive for the average small-scale farmer to use.

## The four pillars

### 1. Agriculture & Food Security
Improves food and nutrition security for children and expectant mothers through better pest and disease control, locally appropriate seeds and breeds, and community nutrition education.

### 2. Economic Development & Employment
Strengthens small and medium enterprises (trade, agribusiness, communications, creative industries) and builds youth entrepreneurship through hands-on training.

### 3. Environment & Climate Change
Protects the natural resources the rest of the project depends on: tree nurseries, solar and biogas energy, drought-tolerant seeds, soil erosion control, rainwater harvesting, and organic production methods.

### 4. Lobbying & Advocacy
Holds local leadership accountable and confronts child exploitation rooted in poverty and tradition — including child labor, child marriage, FGM, and sexual exploitation. This pillar works with local government, law enforcement's children's department, and the community itself to build sustainable, community-owned protection.
